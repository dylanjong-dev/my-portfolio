Portfolio landing page / lead funnel

For a food photographer who had strong craft and a published cookbook, but no clear path to turn followers into income.

I transformed a standard portfolio into a conversion-focused lead funnel, optimizing the UX to guide visitors seamlessly from admiring her work to booking her services.

Lead generation landing page

For a Massachusetts brand making premium, handmade wooden cutting boards. To immediately convey craftsmanship, I paired elegant serif typography with rich, immersive photography.

I designed a story-driven layout that guides visitors from brand heritage to product selection, using strategic trust signals (reviews, FAQs) to reduce friction and drive both direct sales and custom requests.

Tech website

A site for a company offering CRM and AI-powered management solutions. Since tech products can be overwhelming, I focused on simplifying the information architecture.

By designing intuitive pricing tables and clear process flows, visitors can easily digest the features and choose a plan with clarity.

Growth tools webpage

A funnel-style page for a platform-growth software (handling follow-ups, booking sync, etc.).

To reduce bounce rates, I structured the page narrative to directly address user pain points first, followed by clear visual workflows that guide the prospect smoothly toward the conversion goal.

About me

I’m Dylan, a Web & UI/UX designer based in Malaysia. I design websites and landing pages for education, tech, F&B, coaching, real estate, and personal brands, where the design has to fit the brand and make the visitor’s next step obvious. I work across UI/UX, web design, branding, and visual design, and I can take a site from concept to a working build.


I started out as an intern, where I built three company websites from scratch, taking each one from first idea to a live site. Since then I’ve worked across several industries, and each project starts the same way: understand the brand’s personality, then work out the one thing a visitor most needs to do. Style should match the brand, and structure should serve the user’s main goal. That’s what each case study here shows: the brief, the decisions I made, and why. Updates on Facebook.
